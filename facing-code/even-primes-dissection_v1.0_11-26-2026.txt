== 5. Down into scale (2k -> k -> k/2 ...) ==
  every E2 meets the odd line after exactly one halving (v2 = 1): E1 meets its odd prime, E2-only an odd composite.
  next step down: p/2 = w + 1/2 (the wing plus half the centre unit): 14 -> 7 -> 7/2 = 3 + 1/2 ; 18 -> 9 -> 4 + 1/2.
  4k need v2 >= 2 halvings; the depth equals the number of hinge levels in the unified code (centre chain zeros).
  fraction reading 2k <-> 1/k: E1 <-> 1/p, E2 <-> 1/odd; the place product 2ab <-> (1/2)(1/a)(1/b):
   each even multiplication = unit fractions multiplied and halved once (2 as the halving mover).

== 6. The oddity: primality and factoring trade places ==
  odd line: primes thin (odd primes to 5e5: 41537, share 0.08307), factoring is single.
  even line (own product): primes are a fixed quarter of all numbers, gap always 4, no broken side;
   factoring is not single and the number of ways = splits of the odd part into at most v2 pieces.
  first k-way evens: 2 -> 36, 3 -> 180, 4 -> 360, 5 -> 840; max ways to 1e6: 158 at 831600
  way-counts that no even <= 1e6 has: [17, 22, 34, 39, 45, 49, 54, 55, 57, 58, 60, 61] ... (84 of 1..157)
  13 ways only at: [69984, 202500, 777924] (2^5*3^7, 4*3^4*5^4, 4*3^4*7^4)
  The odd line's prime/composite split reappears in the even line only as how many builds a number has:
   2p^1 (E2) 1 build, 4p 1 build, 4pq 2 builds, 4p^2 2 builds; E1 and E2-only look the same from inside the evens.
