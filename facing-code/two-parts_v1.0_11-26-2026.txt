1. THE TIE. Halving k, k+1, k of odd n: wing w = (n-1)/2, centre c = (n+1)/2, and c^2 - w^2 = (c-w)(c+w) = 1 x n.
   A turn face a x b (a <= b, both odd, ab = n) is the SAME shape: n = ((a+b)/2)^2 - ((b-a)/2)^2, centre (a+b)/2, wing (b-a)/2,
   centre - wing = a (the block). The halving picture is the turn face 1 x n read as a square difference (block 1).
   So: pictures of n with centre^2 - wing^2 = n  <->  turn faces of n, one to one. A prime has one face (1 x p), so one picture:
   its halving. An odd composite has one more picture per face; the extra pictures carry its own blocks as centre - wing.
   5: faces [(1, 5)]  pictures (centre, wing) [(3, 2)]  turn 5 x 1
   11: faces [(1, 11)]  pictures (centre, wing) [(6, 5)]  turn 11 x 1

5. THE NUT. Candidate y = p + 2h. Its halving picture is p's halving shifted by h: wing_y = w_p + h, centre_y = c_p + h (proven).

     r | y-1  iff  h = -w_p mod r;   r | y+1  iff  h = -c_p mod r;   r | y (seat closed)  iff  h = -(w_p + c_p) 2^-1 = -p 2^-1 mod r.
